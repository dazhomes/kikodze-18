This is the weaker case, and it is not close
Long-let residential returns 4.5% gross against the office case's 7.5% — on capital that is $50,000 higher, because three kitchens and four bathrooms cost more than open floors. The building is also competing against thousands of Tbilisi apartments rather than a handful of whole buildings.

And it carries a legal condition the office case does not

The building is registered as one cadastral unit — a single-family house. Letting the floors under one title is straightforward. Creating three separately titled apartments is a registration exercise, and it also needs separate metering, acoustic separation and independent access. Until that is confirmed with the registry, this scenario should be underwritten as one owner letting three floors, not as three saleable apartments.

What we would check before you buyIncluding the parts that do not help us sell it

12 / 12
  • Permitted use and change of useThe building is registered as a single-family house. Office occupation needs the permitted use confirmed, and changed if it is not already commercial. This is the gating item for Scenario A and it should be resolved before exchange, not after.
  • Cultural heritage protection zoneMtatsminda and Sololaki sit within a protection zone. Under the Law on Cultural Heritage Protection, works affecting the exterior require Ministry consent. The façade is finished, so this should not bite — but signage, rooflights, air-conditioning units and any external plant for an office fit-out all touch it.
  • The area basis — this is the one that moves the yield474 m² cadastral against ~334 m² net usable is a 42% difference. We model the rent as a monthly building figure precisely so the choice of denominator does not do the work, but a tenant will still negotiate on a rate. Confirm the cadastral and gross internal areas on the extract and the architect's drawings before agreeing any rate per m² with anyone.
  • Title, pledges and the commissioning certificateClean extract, no encumbrances, and the completion/commissioning documents in order. Standard, and we do it on every transaction.
  • A fixed-price fit-out scope$200,000 is a cap we have set, not a quotation we have received. Before exchange we would put a contractor in the building and turn it into a fixed scope and a real number — the grid on slide 6 shows that holding it at $200,000 rather than $150,000 costs about 0.8 points of yield — survivable, but the difference between a 10% asset and a 10.8% one.
  • The exit has no comparableNothing resembling a finished new-build townhouse has traded in central Mtatsminda. The high comparables in this deck are hillside villas with land; the central ones are old stock. Underwrite this on the income, and treat any capital gain as an unpriced option rather than part of the return.
